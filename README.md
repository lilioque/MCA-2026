> 2. Identify a theme for your dataset. This will be the central, cohesive strand that will bring your data together. It can be an artist, composer, songwriter, performer, album, larger work (i.e. opera, musical, etc.), time period, genre, archival grouping, etc. You will want to be sure that you can gather all three types of data (musical scores, metadata, and audio recordings) based on your selected theme. 

The theme of my dataset is a cantata _Carmina Burana_ by Carl Orff, especially its first movement, 'O Fortuna'. It was not my first choice - initially, I thought about Moby or Ramin Djawadi, because I like how they incorporate culturally significant music created by others into their own work. But when I saw Choral Public Domain Library in the recommended datasets, _Carmina Burana_ came to my mind, and I decided that it would probably be easier to find all the necessary data for it in open access. As a bonus, while looking for it on the Choral Public Domain Library (which does not contain Orff's work), I discovered that it is also actually a modern rendition of a medieval material, which aligns with my initial interest in examining music that reuses (and by this, preserves and curates) existing artwork.

> 3. For this week's reflection, consider your own experience with music. What do you think are some challenges to working with music and music-related data? These could be related to issues like access, curation, distribution, etc. How does your selected theme display some of these challenges? Describe the current manifestations of data relating to your selected theme, discussing how it is presently curated (collected and gathered) and how it is presented, described, and analysed. What strengths and weaknesses do you observe about these approaches?

To my mind, the greatest obstacle to working with music is the fact that now most of music is consumed via streaming services, which means that it is not always obvious how to obtain a downloadable version. Even if a person pays for a streaming service, they still need to purchase a file if they want to work with it in some professional software. In relation to my selected theme, that means that I had to adjust my choice to those options that exist in open access. At the same time, recordings of classical music like Carmina Burana by Carl Orff are not always perfectly curated. Since it is not a one-off recording (like it would be for studio recordings of Moby or Ramin Djawadi music), but there exist multiple recordings of performances by different orchestras and choirs, in different concert halls, and made at different points of time, these metadata is imortant and should always go alongside the recording. But, as practice shows, it is not always so. For example, the first recording of O Fortuna I came across when working on this task was on the BBC website (https://www.bbc.co.uk/teach/ten-pieces/articles/zvnrscw), where they somehow maneged to fail to provide the credentials.
