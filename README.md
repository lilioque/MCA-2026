> 2. Identify a theme for your dataset. This will be the central, cohesive strand that will bring your data together. It can be an artist, composer, songwriter, performer, album, larger work (i.e. opera, musical, etc.), time period, genre, archival grouping, etc. You will want to be sure that you can gather all three types of data (musical scores, metadata, and audio recordings) based on your selected theme. 

The theme of my dataset is a cantata _Carmina Burana_ by Carl Orff, especially its first movement, 'O Fortuna'. It was not my first choice - initially, I thought about Moby or Ramin Djawadi, because I like how they incorporate culturally significant music created by others into their own work. But when I saw Choral Public Domain Library in the recommended datasets, _Carmina Burana_ came to my mind, and I decided that it would probably be easier to find all the necessary data for it in open access. As a bonus, while looking for it on the Choral Public Domain Library (which does not contain Orff's work), I discovered that it is also actually a modern rendition of a medieval material, which aligns with my initial interest in examining music that reuses (and by this, preserves and curates) existing artwork.
